# Untitled Plant Breed

## Problem Statement
Very often, when students are studying genetics, it can be difficult to understand the full extent as to how genes behave and blend when parents produce offspring. Despite early on being taught about dominant and nondominant genes, it is still commonly misunderstood that many kinds of genes are a fifty-fifty split in offspring.

## Solution
Nu-User is developing an educational piece of software that simulates the crossbreeding of different species of flowers, permitting users to freely crossbreed different flowers and witness the outcomes. Everytime a crossbreed is produced, the program will walk the user through how each gene participated in creating the offspring, as well as pointing out any discrepancies the user may find.

The goal isn't to create a 100% accurate to real life simulation, where hundreds of real flower breeds can be studied, produced, and experimented with. Rather, the goal is to provide a generalized, hands-on environment where students can observe how genes generally interact with each other, and where students can visually build there understanding and expectations of gene behaviors.

## Target Market
This product is intended not just for students, but for any individual who is curious about studying how genes truly play a part in the production of offspring. This product will provide a visual entry into observing how genes behave, through the visually simple subject of flowers.

## Why is this valuable?
This application provides a virtual, hands-on environment in which students or independent learners are able to experiment with crossbreeding flowers, so that they may develop a stronger understanding of genes through real time examples. On top of that, users will be able to test themselves on their knowledge through practice quizes with questions such as:
- "Given the genes of Plant A and Plant B, would they be able to produce Plant C as an offspring?"
- "Given the genes of Plant A and Plant B, what is the probability of offspring Plant C developing trait X?"
- "Given the genes of Plants 1-4, pick two plants A and B that could produce Plant C as an offspring."
Not only will learners be able to witness the results of crossbreeding flowers, they will also be able to prove their understanding of genetic patterns.

To provide a widely accessible, platform-independent product, this program will be available on a free to access webpage.
